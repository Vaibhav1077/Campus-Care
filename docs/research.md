# Research: Campus Facility Management Systems

## Problem Hypothesis

Students on campus face significant delays and lack of transparency when reporting maintenance and facility issues. Informal reporting channels (verbal complaints, emails to individual staff) result in issues going untracked, unacknowledged, or unresolved for extended periods. A structured digital complaint system can reduce resolution time and improve student satisfaction by creating accountability and visibility.

## Existing Solutions

### 1. FixMyStreet (mySociety, UK)
- **Type:** Civic issue reporting platform (open-source)
- **URL:** https://www.fixmystreet.com
- **Notes:** Allows citizens to report local infrastructure problems (potholes, broken streetlights) on a public map. Good transparency model and simple interface. However, it is designed for public civic infrastructure — it lacks campus-specific workflows such as internal department routing, student authentication, or academic calendar-aware scheduling. Not suitable for a closed institutional environment without significant customisation.

### 2. Freshdesk (Freshworks)
- **Type:** Commercial helpdesk / ticketing SaaS
- **URL:** https://freshdesk.com
- **Notes:** A fully-featured customer support platform with ticket assignment, SLA tracking, canned responses, and reporting dashboards. While technically capable of handling facility complaints, it is expensive for small institutions, requires IT administration, and is over-engineered for a student project context where simplicity and low overhead are priorities. Free tier is limited and cloud-dependent.

## Sources

### Source 1
**Citation:** Surjit, J., & Haron, N. A. (2014). "Maintenance Management System for University Facilities." *International Journal of Sustainable Construction Engineering & Technology*, 5(2), 1–10.
**URL:** https://publisher.uthm.edu.my/ojs/index.php/IJSCET/article/view/908
**Notes:** This paper examines maintenance management practices in university facilities and identifies key gaps: absence of structured reporting channels, delayed responses, and poor record-keeping. Directly supports the problem hypothesis that campus facility complaints require a formal tracking system.

### Source 2
**Citation:** Lam, E. W. M., Chan, A. P. C., & Chan, D. W. M. (2010). "Benchmarking the performance of building maintenance in the higher education sector." *Facilities*, 28(3/4), 134–145. https://doi.org/10.1108/02632771011023416
**Notes:** Published in the Emerald *Facilities* journal (peer-reviewed). This benchmarking study of higher education building maintenance found that institutions lacking digital complaint tracking systems showed lower maintenance performance scores. Supports the case for systematising campus issue reporting.
